THE CREW

Version 3.10 · XAUUSD · MetaTrader 5

Eleven session specialists under one warden. Equal-lot recovery. You choose how many run.

Symbol: XAUUSD (Gold) — any broker spelling (XAUUSD, XAUUSD+, XAUUSD-ECN, GOLD.pro) Timeframe: any — the EA runs its own internal clock · Platform: MetaTrader 5 · Account type: hedging

This manual is written for someone who has never run an expert advisor before. If you only read one chapter, read chapter 4.

1. What This Is

THE CREW runs eleven independent sub-strategies on one gold chart — ten that wake in their own hour window and one, Delphine, that buys sharp dips at any hour. Each opens a single position, and — if price moves against it — adds equal-sized layers at a fixed dollar spacing, closing the whole group when price returns to a shared average target.

The council you choose is the main risk decision. That is unusual and it is the point: you are not tuning an abstract percentage, you are choosing which specialists trade. Some of them are structurally shallow and rarely build a deep recovery; others carry most of the account risk on their own. Section 5 gives you what is currently measured about the difference.

Read this first. This is a grid. It holds positions through adverse moves by design — that is how it earns. You will see floating (unrealised) losses on open trades while a group recovers. Sections 4 and 10 are mandatory reading.

These are backtest figures throughout this manual, not results from live trading. Results differ by broker — the measured reason is in section 4.5. Past performance is not indicative of future results. Only trade with money you can afford to lose.

2. How It Works (Plain Language)

Eleven specialists, different hours. Each sub-strategy trades one narrow window of the day and one direction. Some buy the London pre-open, one sells the morning session, one works the small hours. They spread the work across the day instead of betting on a single idea.

Each one recovers rather than stops out. When a position goes against it, the sub-strategy does not panic-close and does not double down. It adds another position of the same size a measured distance away, which pulls the group's average price toward the market. The whole group closes together once price reaches that shared average target.

Adding layers is the engine, not the emergency. This matters and it is counter-intuitive: a depth cap was tested and made things worse — the group could no longer pull its average toward price, so it sat open far longer and the floating loss grew larger, not smaller. Depth caps are therefore not offered as a risk control on the shipped build, because measured, they are not one.

One bound, placed where recoveries end. Since 3.0 every group carries a stop at a 150 USD gold move per 0.01 lot (InpMaxBasketLossUsd, scaled with the group's lot, read on every tick). It sits beyond the depth normal recoveries reach — groups that recovered never floated past about 120–195 per 0.01 lot in the tests — and catches the rare one-way run (one group in 2022–24 floated to 645 before it came back). It fires about once or twice a year and costs roughly 15% of net; set it to 0 to run the ladder unbounded on a large account.

A shared arbiter stops the specialists from crowding. Three of the eleven trade the same hour in the same direction. When they compete for the same moment, a fixed priority decides which one takes it. This makes the EA behave consistently; see section 8 for what it does and does not buy you.

Flat lots inside a group. Always. Every layer of a group is the same size as the layer that opened it. No martingale, no doubling, no escalation while a recovery is running — risk inside a group grows in a straight line. Between groups the size follows the account, so the strategy grows with the balance (section 5b).

A hard account breaker. If floating drawdown reaches your configured percentage of the equity peak, the EA flattens everything and stands down until the next day. An optional confirmation delay (InpBreakerConfirmSec, default: fire on the first tick) lets you require the breach to persist before it acts.

Never refuses, never overrides. Whatever you configure is what runs - any council on any balance. If the balance cannot carry the chosen council at the chosen risk tier, the journal prints the real floating exposure and the minimum balance at start-up, and the EA trades your configuration anyway.

3. Quick Start

  1. Attach to a gold chart (any timeframe) on a hedging account.
  2. Leave Council on Low risk - 6 strategies (the default) or load a preset from presets/.
  3. Choose your Sizing (section 5b): Compounding (the default — the lot grows with the balance; Conservative budgets 10% of the balance for floating drawdown. On a small account, load THE_CREW_SMALL_ACCOUNT instead: the same council at a fixed 0.01 lot (section 5, Small accounts).
  4. Check the startup line the EA prints in the journal. If your server clock is not GMT+2/+3, set the journal's BROKER CLOCK line: with Broker clock = Auto-detect (the default) THE CREW finds your broker's clock itself and prints the shift it uses; check it says what you expect before you fund anything (section 7).

That is the whole setup. Everything else is already the validated configuration.

4. Where the Risk Really Lives

1. It holds floating drawdown on purpose. While a group recovers you will see red on open trades. That is the mechanism working. If you cannot leave that alone, this is not the right strategy for you.

2. There is no stop loss. The only brake is the account-level breaker, and when it fires it realises the loss it was holding. It is a last resort, not a routine safety net.

3. Your account balance decides how the floating loss feels. The same trades produce a very different experience at different balances, because a fixed dollar amount of floating loss is a smaller share of a larger account. Section 5b hands the arithmetic to the EA, which is what makes the position size grow with the account.

5. Your broker changes the answer. 6. Scheduled news is filtered; unscheduled shocks are the human's job. By default no new groups open on NFP Fridays, FOMC decision days (Federal Reserve published schedule, built in through December 2027), 15 December - 15 January and after 22:00 on Friday; open groups keep their exit. These rules are the only dates in the code - there is no backtest-derived day list. No filter pre-empts a geopolitical spike or an unscheduled announcement: consider flattening by hand when one is coming and you run a wide council. From 2028 the FOMC filter needs an update; the EA says so in the journal.

Small accounts — the group stop and the minimum balance

THE CREW holds floating drawdown on purpose, and on a large account that is the whole design. On a small account one deep group can take the account with it, so a small account needs a bound. The THE_CREW_SMALL_ACCOUNT preset runs Low risk - 6 strategies at a static 0.01 lot and closes any group whose floating loss reaches 150 USD at 0.01 lot (the same gold move on a USD, EUR or cent account), read on every tick. (The 150 stop is on in every council by default.)

Where that 150 comes from. Closing a group early normally turns a recovery into a loss: in our tests, a stop at 100 closed nine groups, and eight of them would have finished in profit within a few hours. Groups that recovered never floated deeper than about 120–195 per 0.01 lot; the one group that went further went much further (about 645 before it came back). A stop at 150 sits past nearly all normal recoveries and still catches that kind of run. Expect roughly one or two stops a year.

What one stop costs: about 150–165 at 0.01 lot — 30–33 % of a 500 account, 15 % of 1,000. Low risk - 6 had none on four records.

The minimum scales with the lot: at 0.02, double it. These are the EA's guidance, not a gate — you can run any preset on any balance, and the EA warns in the journal and trades what you chose.

Running the stop off (InpMaxBasketLossUsd = 0). Measured on the 3.01 crew (8 strategies): on BlackBull 2025–26 the book without the stop earned about 20% more (2,468 vs 2,056 at 0.01 lot from 500) with no realised loss at all: both stopped groups recovered. On 2022–24 it met 2023-03-20: one group floated to 645 per 0.01 lot. With the 30% flatten on, a 500 account took −196 instead of the stop's −152; with the flatten off too, the broker's margin call closed the account at 15. The stop costs a little on good years and is the account on the bad one. Below about 1,200 at 0.01 lot, leave it on.

5b. Risk Tier — how the EA turns section 5 into a lot size

You do not have to size the lot by hand, and you should not be stuck at 0.01 forever while the account grows. That is what the Risk tier does.

Why this works, exactly. This engine is linear in volume: flat lots inside a group, no stop loss, no escalation during a recovery. Trade for trade, doubling the lot doubles the profit and doubles the floating drawdown. Nothing else changes — not the entries, not the spacing, not the depth. So a risk setting can be stated as one honest number: the share of your balance you are willing to see floating. The EA divides that budget by the council's own measured floating drawdown at 0.01 lots and sizes accordingly:

lot = 0.01 x (balance x budget% / 100) / (worst floating drawdown at 0.01 lots, measured
      for your broker type and council, printed in the journal)
Risk tier Float budget Character
Conservative ⭐ 10% the shipped default: slowest growth, calmest ride
Balanced 15% reached a hard flatten once on one live feed (section 4.5c)
High 20% faster growth, more floating drawdown
Extreme 30% ⚠ account blow-up is possible. Risk capital only

Static or compounding — your choice. Sizing = Compounding (the default) sizes every new group from the risk tier above, so the lot grows with the account. Sizing = Static trades the Static lot (InpLot, 0.01 by default) on every group whatever the balance — the floor model: 0.02 lots needs twice the floor and earns twice the profit.

Three things to know before you rely on it.

  1. The lot is locked when a group opens. Every layer of that group uses the size the first position used, even if the balance moves while the recovery runs. It has to be — equal-lot averaging is what pulls the group's average price toward the market, and that is the only way a group closes. Only new groups pick up the new size.
  2. The divisor is measured on the broker type you select, not automatically on yours. Section 4.5 is not a footnote: worst floating exposure varies meaningfully across feeds. Broker type picks which measured table the EA divides by — Standard (the default) or OANDA (which also shifts the clock one hour). Backtest your own broker before you trust either one.
  3. A Custom council has no measured number, so the EA assumes the deepest float ever measured for a crew and sizes small. It says so in the journal.

If the account is too small to reach the requested budget, the EA trades the council you chose at the broker's minimum lot and prints the real share of balance that configuration carries, with the balance that council needs.

Margin, not floating drawdown, can be the binding constraint on a low-leverage account. A free-margin floor (default 20%, InpMinFreeMarginPct, 0 = off) blocks new groups when free margin runs low. It never blocks a layer — blocking layers is a depth cap, and section 2 explains why those make things worse. Set it to 0 to let every group open and leave the decision to the broker.

6. Testing a Small Account on a Large Demo — InpVirtualBalance

Not on the shipped facade. The product build exposes thirty-three inputs and InpVirtualBalance is not one of them; it is fixed at 0 (off). The mechanism is still in the source and appears when the file is compiled with RESEARCH_BUILD defined. On the shipped binary, fund the demo at the balance you actually intend to trade — that is the only configuration the facade can express, and it measures the same thing more honestly. This section documents the mechanism for anyone building from source.

Brokers rarely let you fund a demo with exactly your intended balance, and a large demo will happily size positions a small account could never carry. InpVirtualBalance rebases the whole risk arithmetic onto a fictional balance:

virtual_balance = InpVirtualBalance + (real_balance - real_balance_at_start)
virtual_equity  = InpVirtualBalance + (real_equity  - real_equity_at_start)
  • Position sizing uses the virtual balance, so the demo trades the lots your intended account would actually trade. (Under Sizing = Static the size is your static lot either way.)
  • The account breaker (InpMaxAccountDDPct) and the equity-peak tracking measure against the virtual account.
  • The end-of-run journal line reports worst floating drawdown in money and as a percentage of the virtual balance.
  • 0 disables it entirely.

Use it like this: fund the demo with whatever the broker gives you, set InpVirtualBalance to your intended balance, and sizing, the breaker and the risk report all behave as though the account were that size. The journal will print a line similar in shape to:

THE CREW v3.10 | XAUUSD | roster=Low risk - 6 strategies (6 specialists) | breaker=30.0% | spread<=1.00
THE CREW SIZING: budget 10.0% of <virtual balance> against a measured <X> float per 0.01 lot
        -> <lot> lots. Lots follow the balance.
THE CREW: VIRTUAL BALANCE <virtual balance> (real equity <real equity>) - sizing, the breaker and
        the risk report all measure against the virtual account.
THE CREW RISK: worst floating drawdown <-X> = <Y>% of the VIRTUAL balance <virtual balance>

<X> is the figure measured for your own broker type and council — read it from your own journal rather than assume a number from this manual. That is the only honest way to see how the strategy would feel on the account you intend to trade.

7. Settings Reference

The shipped build has thirty-three inputs, and the engine reads every one of them — there is no switch that does nothing. Where an input only matters in a certain setup, its label says so (for example Static lot (Sizing = Static)). The presets load the tested configurations; anything you change runs exactly as set — the EA warns in the journal, it never overrides. What stays fixed are each specialist's internal constants (entry gate, ladder spacing, target, depth), because they interlock and have no meaning one at a time.

(The full surface still exists in the same source file. Uncomment #define RESEARCH_BUILD near the top of the .mq5 and recompile and every fixed value becomes an input again — one source of truth, so a research finding and a shipped build can never disagree about what the engine does. Research .set files are not loadable by the shipped binary, and vice versa.)

How to read the risk column. 2. Broker — set once

Input Default What it does
Broker clock (InpClockMode) Auto-detect Every specialist trades at a fixed hour of the clock THE CREW was validated on (the GMT+2/+3 server most brokers run). Auto-detect finds your broker's clock by itself: gold pauses for one hour every day at 17:00 New York, and THE CREW looks for that quiet hour in the last 8 days of prices — on the validated clock it starts at 00:00, so where it sits on your server tells THE CREW how many hours to shift. With Broker clock on Manual, a wrong clock does not degrade the edge visibly — it removes it silently, because the specialists are calibrated to market moments and an hour only names a moment if the clock agrees. The response is a cliff, not a slope, and there is no safe direction to err in. Check the startup line the EA prints in the journal before you fund anything.

7. Chart

Input Default What it does

Still fixed inside each specialist: the scoped arbiter, the one-minute evaluation clock, the entry filter, and every ladder constant (spacing, target, maximum depth) — each specialist keeps its own. None of these is a preference; each is a measurement.

Settings we deliberately do NOT offer as risk controls

Both were tested and both increase risk when set tight:

  • Depth cap. Capping the deepest specialist's layers moved its worst floating drawdown substantially deeper and stretched one group's hold time from hours to weeks. Adding layers is what lets a group close.
  • A tight floating-loss stop. Closing a group once it is underwater by a small fixed amount converts recoverable groups into realised losses: at 100 per 0.01 lot, eight of nine stopped groups would have finished ahead. The group stop (InpMaxBasketLossUsd) is therefore set beyond the depth normal recoveries reach — 150 per 0.01 lot, on by default.

The one place the EA will refuse to act is the free-margin guard (20% by default) — and it refuses to open a new group, never to add a layer to an existing one, precisely because of the first bullet. A third rejected control is ATR-denominated spacing: widening the ladder in a volatile regime starves the grid of the layers it needs to pull its average toward price, and measured it cost a large share of net while making results more variable across regimes, not less. For a recovery grid, high volatility calls for more layers, not wider ones.

7b. The Status Panel and the Chart Lines

With Status panel on (the default), THE CREW draws a panel in the chart's top-left corner and keeps it current every second. It shows what the engine itself uses — nothing on it is an estimate:

  • Header — council, sizing (static 0.01 lot per group, or compounding 10% → next group 0.07 lot), the server time and the broker-clock shift (auto or manual).
  • Breaker — the account equity at which the 30% breaker would flatten THE CREW, and the equity now. If it has fired: no new groups until the next day.
  • Other trades on XAUUSD — shown only when another EA (or you) holds gold on the same account: those lots move the equity the breaker reads and use the same margin (section 7c).
  • One row per specialist — name, risk class, its trading hours on your server clock, and what it is doing: in window – watching, next window 22:00, not today, paused (news / holiday), quiet window (US release) (Delphine), stood down.
  • An open group takes two lines: BUY or SELL, layers × lot, the floating result, then its target, the price of the next layer (or ladder full) and the group stop.

On the chart, each open group gets three short lines, labelled with the specialist's name and the price:

Line Colour Meaning
target green, dashed the whole group closes here (its average price + the specialist's target)
next layer blue (buy) / orange (sell), dotted the next equal-lot layer opens when the ask (buy) or bid (sell) reaches it
group stop red, dash-dot where the group's loss reaches the group stop at its current layers. The stop is a dollar amount, so every new layer moves this line closer

THE CREW opens its layers at market on its own one-minute clock — there are no pending orders to see on the chart, which is why it draws the levels itself. The panel never draws in a normal (non-visual) Strategy Tester run, and it is removed with the EA.

7c. Running THE CREW Beside Another EA

THE CREW can share an account — and the gold symbol — with another EA (for example KINGFISHER). Put it on its own XAUUSD chart. It recognises its own trades by their magic numbers (89401–89412) and never touches another EA's positions. Three things are shared, and you should set THE CREW up knowing them:

  1. Sizing. Under Sizing = Compounding the lot follows the whole account balance, including the other EA's capital and profits. To give THE CREW its own slice of the account, use Sizing = Static and set the lot for that slice (section 5, Small accounts: about 0.01 lot per 500–1,000 depending on your appetite).
  2. The 30% breaker reads the whole account equity — the other EA's open trades, its profits and any broker credit included. It closes only THE CREW's groups. Credit raises the equity peak, so with credit the breaker fires later in terms of your own money.
  3. Margin. Both EAs draw on the same free margin; THE CREW opens no new group below 20% free margin.

Before 3.09 THE CREW's long-exposure cap (Max open long ounces, 24 oz) counted every gold position on the account, so a second gold EA holding 0.24 lot or more long would have blocked THE CREW's new buy groups. From 3.09 the cap counts THE CREW's own positions only.

8. The Arbiter — and what it does not do

Three specialists trade the same hour in the same direction. The arbiter is fixed at Scoped: only the highest-priority one takes a contested moment, scoped so that specialists which never share an hour never interfere. It is not a setting in the shipped build.

It is a consistency feature, not a safety feature. Measured across every arbiter setting, the worst equity drawdown was the same — because the deepest drawdown belongs to a specialist the arbiter never suppresses. It costs a small share of net. It ships on because it makes behaviour predictable, not because it reduces risk. If you want less risk, use a narrower council or a lower risk tier.

9. What to Expect Day to Day

  • Most groups close quickly, typically within minutes to a few hours.
  • Most groups never add a single layer.
  • A small share of groups go several layers deep. Those are the ones that matter for sizing.
  • Deep groups usually resolve within a few hours; occasionally one runs for days.
  • Long flat stretches are normal — most sub-strategies trade only in their own hour window.

11. Risk Disclosure

Trading leveraged instruments carries substantial risk of loss. THE CREW is a grid strategy without a stop loss: it holds losing positions through adverse moves and recovers them by adding equal-sized layers. This exposes the account to open-ended floating loss during a sustained adverse trend.

All performance figures in this manual are backtest results on historical data, produced in the MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester on real-tick history over a period in which gold trended strongly upward. They are not results from live trading and not a prediction. No bear-market evidence exists for this strategy. Broker conditions materially change the outcome. Never trade money you cannot afford to lose, and never run a grid on an account you cannot leave alone through a recovery.

Is it waiting, or is it broken? — the regime monitor

This product earns in a particular market state and goes quiet outside it. That is disclosed above, but a disclosure without a number is not usable: meeting a flat stretch, you cannot tell whether the machine is waiting for its conditions or whether its edge has changed. This section gives you the one measurement that separates those, taken from this product's own record.

The state to watch is the daily Average True Range of the traded instrument, expressed as a percentile of its own trailing 250 days — not a fixed number of dollars or pips, which goes stale as the instrument reprices. Most charting packages will plot ATR; the percentile is simply where today's ATR sits within the last year of its own values.

Not yet measured on this product. The tripwire requires a real-tick record of at least 36 months so the good-state sample can bound anything; the validation runs behind this manual are shorter than that. Rather than print a number from a sample too thin to support it, this section states the method and leaves the threshold open.

What to do in the meantime. Track the daily ATR percentile as described above alongside your monthly result. A flat stretch while the percentile is low is the behaviour this manual already describes. A flat stretch while the percentile is high is the case worth reporting, because that is the combination that would indicate a change rather than a wait.

What this cannot tell you. While the market is outside the good state, a temporary quiet period and a permanent change in behaviour look identical. The tripwire is the earliest point at which they separate, not an early warning. Nothing here is a guarantee; it is the historical extreme of a backtested record, and being beyond it is evidence rather than proof.
