Jab is a MetaTrader 5 (MT5) Expert Advisor that runs a level-to-level scalping strategy on gold (XAUUSD). It rests a stop order at the freshest confirmed swing pivot on the M15 chart and is filled the instant price reaches it, then manages the position with a short, two-stage trailing stop. It does not predict direction; it waits at a place on the chart and takes the fill when the market brings the price to it.

How a trade is made:

  1. Locate the freshest confirmed swing pivot on M15 that is far enough from the current price and has not already been tested more than once since it formed.
  2. Rest a stop order just beyond that pivot, in the direction of the last closed candle.
  3. Cancel the order if it has not filled within its patience window.
  4. On a fill, move the stop once the trade is a set distance ahead.
  5. Once the trade is a further set distance ahead, switch to a tight trail and give back as little of the move as possible.
  6. Every order carries a hard stop loss from the moment it is placed.

Jab holds at most one position per side. It does not average down, does not use a grid, and does not use martingale position sizing. A losing trade is closed at its stop and Jab moves on.

Why the level is chosen this way: the pivot Jab rests on is picked for freshness, not for being the nearest price to the current one, and a pivot that has already been tested more than once is skipped. Measured against seven other ways to define "the level" - the candle body instead of the wick, a cluster of nearby pivots, the prior trading session's high or low, a statistical band drawn from the pivot candle itself, and the nearest qualifying pivot regardless of age - the freshest untested wick extreme was the strongest or tied-strongest order price on every measure tried, across several live and demo broker feeds. The timing of the fill carries the same weight as the level itself: resting the order to fill at first touch, rather than waiting for a confirming candle close or a retest of the level, is where the measured edge lives; waiting for confirmation is not a cautious version of the same trade, it gave the edge back on every feed tested.

The two-stage trailing stop shipped here was measured against a faster, tighter exit and against a separate genetic-search pick, on every live broker feed available. The shipped exit was the strongest or tied-strongest arm on the measure that matters for a compounding account - the number of days between one equity high and the next, and the worst of any rolling three-month stretch. The faster exits produced a higher win rate on paper, because they cap the winning trades far below the stop, and that trade-off lost money once real broker spread was in the numbers. Scaling the stop and target to the day's own volatility was tested the same way and dropped: on every live feed it measured, it cost money and made the account's worst stretches less predictable, not more.

Jab shares its engine lineage with two related builds. Heavy Jab runs the same level-to-level mechanism on bitcoin, with its own stop and target distances measured from scratch for that market; an earlier attempt to simply resize Jab's gold distances for bitcoin traded far fewer trades at a loss, which is why Heavy Jab is a separate build rather than a rescaled copy. Jab Savant adds a trained model that screens each qualifying level and sits out the ones its scoring expects to fail; Jab, this build, takes every level that passes its own gates with no learned filter.

Requirements

  • A raw-spread or ECN account is required. Jab takes small, frequent profits, and the same configuration measured on four different live broker feeds produced net results that differed by several multiples on an almost identical number of trades - the per-trade edge is smaller than the gap in cost between brokers, so the account's spread decides more of the outcome than any input on this list.
  • Gold (XAUUSD or the broker's equivalent), M15 chart.
  • Hedging account.
  • VPS recommended; Jab manages its stop on every tick.

Settings

Runs on its defaults with no set file; the compiled defaults are the tested configuration. Inputs are grouped as Money, Risk, Gating, Entry, Exit, Calendar and Diagnostics. The default is a fixed 0.01 lot; set "Use fixed lot" to false to size from a risk percentage instead. An optional named preset, Cadence, loosens the entry gates to trade more often; it is documented in the user manual and is not the default because it depends on a tight-spread broker and a market regime that has not held for the whole tested history.

Prop-firm rules. A second input group takes your firm's own numbers: daily loss limit (a share of the account size or of the day-start value, counted from balance, equity or the higher of the two), maximum loss limit (fixed or trailing), profit target, best-day cap and a Friday close hour. When a limit is about to be reached Jab closes its own trades, deletes its orders and pauses until the firm's next day (daily rules) or stops for good (maximum loss, target); the Journal names the rule and the equity. Every rule is off until a number is entered, and with every rule off Jab trades exactly as it does above. It is a rule guard, not a guarantee: a price gap can carry a loss past a limit, which is why Jab acts half a percent early by default.

Frequently asked questions

Q: Which broker and account type does Jab need? A: A raw-spread or ECN account is required, on a hedging account. Jab takes small, frequent profits, so the account's spread decides a large share of the result - choose a tight-spread broker.

Q: What is the minimum deposit? A: 1,000 in account currency is the recommended minimum. It can trade from a 100 balance, but the manual's deposit ladder recommends 500 or more for a comfortable size relative to the fixed lot.

Q: Which chart and timeframe do I attach it to? A: An XAUUSD (gold) M15 chart. No .set file is needed - the compiled defaults are the validated configuration.

Q: Which risk setting should I start with? A: Use fixed lot (on by default, at a fixed 0.01 lot) is the validated starting point; switch it off to size from a risk percentage per trade instead, and raise the lot only as the balance grows.

Q: Does Jab use a grid or martingale? A: No. It holds at most one position per side, never averages down, and does not use a grid or martingale sizing. Every order carries a hard stop loss from the moment it is placed.

Q: Why does Jab only place a modest number of trades? A: It waits for a freshly confirmed swing pivot that has not already been tested more than once, and skips levels that do not qualify. The optional Cadence preset loosens the entry gates to trade more often, but it is not the default because it depends on a tight-spread broker.

Q: Do I need a VPS? A: A VPS is recommended. Jab manages its trailing stop on every tick, so it needs to keep running once a position is open.

Q: Can I use Jab on a prop-firm account? A: Yes. A prop-firm rules group takes your firm's own daily loss, maximum loss, profit target and Friday-close numbers; every rule is off until a number is entered, and with every rule off Jab trades exactly as described above.
