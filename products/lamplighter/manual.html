LAMPLIGHTER - user manual

Version 1.01 - XAUUSD - MetaTrader 5 - DRAFT (the results by risk level are not in yet: that place is marked [CLAIM: ...])

The Lamplighter walks the same round on gold every day and lights four lamps, each at its hour. He lights the first when the morning range is drawn, the second when London opens, the third where a swing level gives way and the fourth where price comes back to a recent extreme. Every lamp has a stop loss. When the street is too quiet he leaves a lamp dark.

This manual is written for someone who has never run an expert advisor before. If you only read one chapter, read chapter 3a: it says what a small account can expect.

1. What it does, in plain words

Lamplighter is four small strategies on one gold chart. Each is a "lamp": it acts at its own time or at its own level, and has its own stop loss, its own orders and its own magic number. They share no positions and no stops.

Lamp Strategy What it watches When it acts How a trade ends
1 Morning Breakout the range of the early session 06:05 server time: a buy stop above the range, a sell stop below it; when one fills the other is cancelled stop loss at a fixed percentage of price; breakeven once the trade has moved by the size of the session range, then the stop follows price; closed at 17:55 server time if still open
2 London Open the overnight range 09:55 server time: a buy stop and a sell stop around the range stop loss at the middle of the range, target the full range; once in profit the stop locks most of the best profit reached
3 Swing Breakout the nearest unbroken 1-hour swing high above price and swing low below it continuously: one stop order on each side stop loss at a fixed percentage of price; the stop follows price once the trade is ahead; an early false-breakout exit closes a trade that does not follow through within minutes
4 Pullback a 4-day high and a 4-day low once a day, early in the server day, on days when the extreme qualifies stop loss at a fixed percentage of price; the stop follows price once the trade is ahead

A swing is an hourly bar whose high or low is more extreme than the bars on both sides of it. "Unbroken" means no later hourly bar has traded through it.

Every order and every position carries a stop loss from the moment it exists. Each order also carries a take profit; its distance is part of the strategy and is not an input. There is no grid, no martingale and no averaging into a losing position: a losing trade is closed at its stop and nothing is added to it. The product contains no grid or martingale code at all; it is not switched off, it is not there.

Normally each lamp holds one position. Swing Breakout and Pullback rest one order on each side, so in a whipsaw each of them can end up holding a buy and a sell together. Up to six positions can therefore be open at once; in the backtests the most open at one time was four (backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks).

Lamplighter needs a hedging account. The four lamps keep separate positions, and two of them can hold a buy and a sell on gold at the same time. A netting account (one position per symbol) would merge them into one. Chapter 2 says more.

Server time. All session times are broker server times, for a server on GMT+2 in winter and GMT+3 in summer. If your broker's clock is different, chapter 10 shows the three inputs that move the strategies to the right moments.

Two protections that keep a lamp dark. Quiet-market protection (on by default) keeps Morning Breakout out on a day when the recent daily range is small compared with price. London Open stands aside on a day when the overnight range is so wide that a stop at its middle would risk too much of price.

2. Installation (five minutes)

  1. If you bought Lamplighter from the Market, it appears in the Navigator under Market. If you have the file, open MetaTrader 5, File -> Open Data Folder, open MQL5\Experts and copy LAMPLIGHTER.ex5 there; then right-click the Navigator panel and choose Refresh.
  2. Open a gold chart on a hedging account: XAUUSD, XAUUSD+, XAUUSDp, GOLD.pro or whatever your broker calls spot gold against USD. Any timeframe works; the strategies read their own bars.
  3. Drag LAMPLIGHTER onto the chart. On the Common tab tick Allow Algo Trading. On the Inputs tab only section 1. Start here needs you (chapter 2a); the defaults are meant to be run as they are. Click OK.
  4. Make sure the Algo Trading button in the toolbar is green. A smiling face in the top-right corner of the chart means the EA is running.
  5. Check your broker's clock (chapter 4, step 2).

The journal (Toolbox -> Experts) shows one line on start, for example:

LAMPLIGHTER v1.01 | Symbol=XAUUSD TF=PERIOD_M15 | S1=1 S2=1 S3=1 S4=1 | magics 89860..89863 | GMT=off shift=0 | risk RISK_MEDIUM | account stop 20.0% | quiet-market protection 1.20%

It names the symbol and chart timeframe, which of the four strategies are on (1 = on), the four magic numbers, whether the broker-clock adjustment is on and by how many hours it shifts the session times right now, the risk level, the account stop in percent and the quiet-market protection threshold in percent of price (a zero in either place means that protection is off).

Account type: hedging. A hedging account lets one symbol hold several positions at once, including a buy and a sell together. Lamplighter needs that: on a netting account the positions of the four lamps would merge, and a stop or an exit would no longer belong to the lamp that opened the trade. The EA does not check the account type for you. If you are not sure which type you have, ask your broker before you attach it.

2a. The inputs at a glance

The Inputs tab is ordered by how often you should touch it. Most users change only section 1.

Section What it holds Touch it?

2. Strategies switches for the four lamps, and the quiet-market protection threshold rarely
3. Broker clock the session-time adjustment for a server that is not on GMT+2 / GMT+3 only if your broker's clock differs

Maximum spread is in price units; for gold that is dollars. A new order waits while the spread is wider (chapter 6).

Account drawdown stop closes everything and stops the EA when the account falls the set percentage below its high point. The chapter on the account stop explains how.

3. The only setting that matters: risk level

InpRisk is one choice: Low, Medium (the default), High, Hot or Custom. It sets the lot per order from your balance:

Level Lot rule
Low 0.01 lot per 8,000 of balance
Medium (default) 0.01 lot per 4,000 of balance
High 0.01 lot per 2,000 of balance
Hot 0.01 lot per 1,000 of balance - drawdowns can exceed 20 %; for experienced users, with risk capital only
Custom a fixed lot, or a balance per 0.01 lot of your own (section 4 of the Inputs tab)

The lot grows one step (0.01 on a broker whose lot step is 0.01) for every whole block of the balance, and never falls below the broker's minimum. What that means in lots:

balance Low Medium High Hot
1,000 0.01 0.01 0.01 0.01
2,000 0.01 0.01 0.01 0.02
5,000 0.01 0.01 0.02 0.05
10,000 0.01 0.02 0.05 0.10
20,000 0.02 0.05 0.10 0.20

On a small account every level therefore trades the same minimum lot, and the level changes nothing until the balance grows. The lot is the same for all four strategies. The Maximum lot per order input (5.0 by default) caps every single order at every level; it does not cap the total of open lots.

Recommended deposit. 4,000 for the default Medium level, which trades 0.01 lot per 4,000 of balance. The other levels follow the same rule: Low 8,000, High 2,000 and Hot 1,000 per 0.01 lot. Below the balance of the level you chose, the EA trades the broker's minimum lot (chapter 3a says what that means for the dips).

Not gold. The sizing is built for gold. On a symbol that is not gold the EA trades the broker's minimum lot whatever the risk level, and it prints one line in the Experts tab saying it is built for gold.

This is a lot per balance, not a risk percentage. The stops are a percentage of price (or a share of a measured range), so one stop on a given lot costs more when gold is expensive. The panel's Risk now line shows what every open stop would cost in your account currency (chapter 5).

What the testing showed at each level. [CLAIM: growth, profit factor and deepest dip for Low, Medium and High, with broker, window, deposit and execution delay - from the product battery]

Higher levels grow the account faster in a good market and cut it deeper in a bad one. There is no level that avoids that trade-off. Choose the level whose dips you could sit through (chapter 3a).

Custom sizing

Set Risk level = Custom, then either: - Fixed lot - leave "Sizing" on Fixed lot and give the lot in "Fixed lot" (0.01 by default), or - Scale with balance - set "Sizing" to Scale with balance and give the balance per 0.01 lot.

3a. Minimum balance - read this if your account is small

Lamplighter has no minimum balance of its own. Two limits decide how small is too small, and neither is a setting of the EA.

Limit 1 - how deep the dips go.

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, every trade re-expressed at gold 4,100, BlackBull 2022-11..2026-10 / VT Markets 2024-03..2026-10.

At the minimum lot of 0.01, the deepest dip from any start day was:

starting balance BlackBull 2022-11..2026-10 (includes the quiet 2023 market) VT Markets 2024-03..2026-10 (the volatile years only)
2,000 about 42 % of the balance about 13 %
1,000 about 82 % about 27 %

In money, the dip was about 840 per 0.01 lot on BlackBull (window including 2023) and about 270 per 0.01 lot on VT Markets (2024-03..2026-10); a balance smaller than the dip is wiped out. A 500 account was wiped out in the 2023-type market (BlackBull, same window). The dip is measured below the running peak of the account, not below the starting balance (backtest, 0.01 lot, BlackBull 2022-11..2026-10: equity marked every minute at the worst side of each bar; backtest, 0.01 lot, VT Markets 2024-03..2026-10: closed trades).

"Every trade re-expressed at gold 4,100" means each historical trade was rescaled to what it would have been at a gold price of 4,100, so a 0.01-lot trade means the same in money in every year of the window. In practice each trade's money was multiplied by 4,100 divided by its entry price, because the stops and targets are a percentage of price. These dips are for 0.01 lot, which is what Low, Medium and High trade on a 2,000 balance and what every level trades on a 1,000 balance; Hot trades 0.02 on 2,000. Read the two columns together: the volatile years alone look far gentler than a window that includes a quiet year, and a quiet year can come again.

Limit 2 - the broker's margin. One 0.01 lot of gold is one ounce on a broker with a 100-ounce lot, and its margin is the gold price divided by your leverage. At leverage 1:30 (the EU retail limit for gold) and gold near 4,100, one 0.01 position needs about 137 of margin. Up to six positions can be open at once, because Swing Breakout and Pullback can each hold a buy and a sell at the same time, next to one position each for Morning Breakout and London Open; six positions need about 820. In the backtests the most open at one time was four, about 550 (backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks). Below roughly 820 at 1:30 a full set of six positions cannot be carried. Before it sends an order the EA checks that your free margin covers the order plus a 10 % buffer; an order that fails the check is not sent, and the Experts tab says why (chapter 8). At leverage 1:500 the same position needs about 8.

The account stop on a small account. The stop is measured from the highest value the account has reached while the EA runs, not from your starting balance. A 2,000 account that grows to 3,000 and then falls to 2,400 stands twenty percent below its high point, and the stop closes everything and halts the EA although the account is still above where it began. On a small account this can happen early, and after it happens the EA stays off until you press Resume on the panel (chapter 3b). Setting the stop to 0 removes that behaviour and also removes the protection.

Recommended deposit. 4,000 for the default Medium level, which is where the lot is 0.01 per 4,000 of balance (chapter 3). The other levels read: Low 8,000, High 2,000, Hot 1,000 per 0.01 lot. On a balance below that the lot cannot fall under the broker's minimum, so the dips above take a larger share of the account, not a smaller one. For scale: at 0.01 lot the deepest dip of about 840 is about 21 % of 4,000 (BlackBull 2022-11..2026-10, includes the quiet 2023 market), and the dip of about 270 is about 7 % of 4,000 (VT Markets 2024-03..2026-10, the volatile years only); both are the stamped figures above divided by 4,000. Choose the balance for the dip you could sit through, not for a good month.

3b. The account drawdown stop

InpAccountDDPct (20 by default, 0 = off).

  • The EA keeps the highest account equity it has seen since it was started. Equity is balance plus the open profit and loss.
  • When equity is the set percentage or more below that peak, the EA closes all of its trades, deletes all of its orders and stops. The panel's status word reads Stopped, and the journal says: LAMPLIGHTER: account drawdown stop reached - every trade closed, the EA stopped.
  • It measures the whole account's equity, so losses on other EAs or by hand count toward it; it closes only this EA's trades.
  • It keeps its state through a terminal restart. On a live account the EA saves the stop's state and the equity peak; after a restart the panel still reads Stopped, and the journal line restart: saved state restored ends with account drawdown stop TRIPPED - press Resume to clear it. A restart does not clear the stop and does not restart the peak.
  • Resume clears it. Press Resume on the panel: the peak restarts at the current equity and each lamp places again at its next check. If the left button still reads Pause after the stop acted, press it once; it then reads Resume. The buttons are part of the panel, so keep Show the control panel (InpPanel) switched on.
  • 0 switches the stop off: nothing is closed by the account rule.
  • It is a guard, not a guarantee: a price gap can carry the loss past the line before the closing orders fill.

4. Your first week

  1. Run it on a demo account first, same broker and same settings, for at least four weeks. Nothing in this manual replaces watching it on your own broker's prices.
  2. Check the clock on the first morning. The Morning Breakout orders should appear at 06:05 on the server clock shown in Market Watch, and the London Open orders at 09:55. If they appear at another time, your server is not on GMT+2 / GMT+3: see chapter 10.
  3. Each day, glance at the panel (chapter 5): which lamps lit, which stayed dark and why (the guard strip says), and what Risk now reads before and after a fill.
  4. Expect days with no trade at all and weeks where only one or two lamps lit. That is the guards working.
  5. When you go live, use the level that fits your balance (chapter 3 and 3a) and raise the level only after the first weeks match what you saw on demo.

5.2 What Lamplighter draws on the chart (InpDrawOrders, on by default)

  • Resting orders: a dotted line at each order's price, with a label at the right edge of the chart such as Morning buy 4210.50, in blue for a buy and orange for a sell.
  • Open trades: a dashed gold line at the trade's current stop. Hover over it to read the strategy and what that stop does: stop -30.10 (it would lose that much), stop at entry, or stop keeps +12.00 (it would still lock that much). The line moves as the stop follows price.
  • A day without a trade: a small note at the lamp's time when a guard kept it out, quiet day - no Morning trade or wide range - no London trade.

The order and position lines with their stop loss and take profit are MetaTrader's own (switch on Show trade levels in the chart properties if you do not see them). Turning the drawings off changes nothing in the trading.

7. Exit mechanics

A trade can end at its stop loss, at a stop that has been moved (breakeven, a lock or a trail), at its take profit, at the 17:55 close (Morning Breakout only) or at the false-breakout exit (Swing Breakout only).

  • Morning Breakout. Stop loss at a fixed percentage of price. Once the trade has moved by the size of that morning's session range, the stop goes to breakeven; later the stop follows price, set again at the start of each new one-minute bar. Any Morning trade still open at 17:55 server time is closed.
  • London Open. Stop loss at the middle of the overnight range, target the full range. Once the trade is in profit, the stop locks most of the best profit reached, updated on every tick.
  • Swing Breakout. Stop loss at a fixed percentage of price. Once the trade is ahead, the stop follows price, set again at the start of each new one-minute bar. A few minutes after the entry the EA checks whether the trade has followed through; if it has not, the trade is closed.
  • Pullback. Stop loss at a fixed percentage of price. Once the trade is ahead, the stop follows price, set again at the start of each new one-minute bar.

A stop is only ever moved in the trade's favour. The stop loss of every trade is on the broker's server; the moves described above are made by the EA, so the terminal has to be running for them.

8. Execution quality - what your broker adds

Lamplighter rests stop orders, which the broker's server fills the instant price reaches them. Two things from your broker decide how close the fills are to the plan.

  • Spread. The spread is paid on every entry. A wider spread takes a larger share of each stop. The spread guard only stops new orders from being placed while the spread is wide.
  • Fills in fast markets. In the second a major data release comes out, price can jump and the spread can widen, and a resting stop order can fill beyond its price and its stop beyond the stop. Lamplighter has no news filter. The tester's stored ticks may not contain the widest release-second spreads, so a backtest can understate this cost.

Prefer a broker with tight gold spreads and fast order execution, and a VPS close to the broker's server.

The safe trade layer

Before the EA sends any request, it checks that the broker's server would accept it. A request that would fail is not sent. The trading rules do not change: a request the server would have refused is simply not made. The checks are:

  • the market is open for the symbol, and the symbol allows trading (not disabled, and not close-only for a new trade);
  • the volume is inside the broker's minimum and maximum and inside the symbol's volume limit;
  • free margin covers the order plus a 10 % buffer;
  • the account's limit on pending orders is not reached;
  • a stop order sits on the correct side of price, outside the broker's stop level and freeze level;
  • a stop loss, a take profit, or a move of either, is not closer to price than the broker's stop level and freeze level.

When a request is held back, the Experts tab shows one line, safe trade layer: <reason> - request not sent (shown once a day), once a day for each kind of reason. The reason tells you whether it is the account (margin, the pending-order limit) or the broker (stop level, volume limit, market hours).

9. Sizing and margin

  • The lot per order comes from the risk level and the balance (chapter 3). It is the same for all four strategies.
  • There is no cap on the total of open lots, only on each order (Maximum lot per order).
  • Margin per 0.01 lot of gold is about the gold price divided by your leverage. Up to six positions can be open at once (four at most in the backtests). Chapter 3a gives the 1:30 and 1:500 figures.
  • The recommended deposit is 4,000 for the default Medium level (0.01 lot per 4,000); on a smaller balance the EA trades the broker's minimum lot (chapter 3 and 3a).
  • On a symbol that is not gold the EA trades the broker's minimum lot whatever the risk level, and prints one line saying it is built for gold.
  • Before every order the safe trade layer checks free margin with a 10 % buffer (chapter 8). An order that the account cannot carry is not sent, and the reason shows in the Experts tab. The EA does not shrink an order to fit.
  • Use one chart per gold symbol. Two copies on the same symbol with the same magic number would manage each other's orders; a second copy needs a magic number base at least four apart.

10. Broker clock and optimising

The broker clock

The session times (06:05, 09:55 and the 17:55 close) are for a server on GMT+2 in winter and GMT+3 in summer, with the summer switch on the European daylight-saving dates (the last Sunday of March to the last Sunday of October). For any other server:

  1. Switch on Adjust the session times.
  2. Enter your server's offset from GMT in winter and in summer.

The strategies then fire at the same moments of the real day. Example: a server on GMT+0 in winter and GMT+1 in summer: enter 0 and 1. Morning Breakout is then placed at 04:05 server time instead of 06:05, which is the same real moment. A server on a fixed GMT+3 all year: enter 3 in both boxes. The start line in the journal shows the shift in use (shift=...).

Optimising (for advanced users)

Every input in the complete reference below can be searched in the Strategy Tester's optimiser. The strategy constants (the times of day, the stop distances, the breakeven and trailing rules) are fixed in the compiled product and are not inputs. Two warnings:

  • The defaults are the product's configuration. A value that looks better on one quarter of history is usually curve-fitting.
  • Optimise on real ticks ("Every tick based on real ticks"), never on bars: the fills depend on the tick path.

11. What is saved and what is not

On a live account Lamplighter saves its daily state in terminal global variables, and restores it when it starts again. The state is refreshed about once a second while ticks arrive, and at every Pause, Resume and account stop. A terminal restart therefore does not place a day's straddles twice and does not forget a tripped account stop. The journal says restart: saved state restored when it has done so.

  • Saved and restored after a restart:
  • which lamps have already placed their orders today, so a restart inside a lamp's placement hour does not place that day's orders again;
  • Morning Breakout's session range, which the breakeven step measures in, so a Morning trade that is open across a restart still gets its breakeven step;
  • London Open's starting risk and the best profit an open trade has reached, which its locking stop measures from;
  • the account drawdown stop: the equity peak and whether the stop has acted (it stays Stopped until Resume);
  • the Pause from the panel, in a terminal global variable named LLP<magic base>_pause (LLP89860_pause by default).
  • Open orders and open positions are re-adopted after a restart by their magic number.
  • Not saved: nothing is saved in the Strategy Tester; every tester run starts clean.
  • The saved state is keyed by the magic number base, so two copies with different bases keep separate states (chapter 9).
