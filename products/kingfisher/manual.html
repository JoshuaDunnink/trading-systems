KINGFISHER — user manual

Version 1.11 · XAUUSD · MetaTrader 5 · THE AEGIS ARCADE

KINGFISHER sits still above the water and dives once, when the fish breaks the surface. On a gold chart it waits at the nearest price level that has not been broken yet, rests a small order just inside it, and is filled at the moment the level gives way. Every trade has a fixed stop loss and take profit from the second it opens. When the market is too quiet for that stop loss to make sense, KINGFISHER does not trade at all.

This manual is written for someone who has never run an expert advisor before. If you only read one chapter, read chapter 3.

1. What it does, in plain words

Gold moves in bursts. Before a burst, price usually sits under a recent high or above a recent low that it has not managed to break. A lot of traders have stop orders waiting just beyond those levels. When price finally breaks through, those stops fire and the move accelerates for a moment.

KINGFISHER places its own order a hair inside that level, so it is already in the trade when the crowd's orders go off. Five "hunters" do this at the same time:

Hunter Watches Patience
Dive 1 the 4-hour chart's nearest unbroken high and low 30 hours
Dive 2 the same 4-hour level, slightly different distance 20 hours
Dive 3 the same 4-hour level, tight on the buy side 60 hours
Dive 4 the same 4-hour level, wide on both sides 55 hours
Skim the 1-hour chart's nearest unbroken high and low 15 hours

The four Dives rest on the same level and usually fill on the same break — that is deliberate, and it is why KINGFISHER sizes risk per level, not per order (chapter 3).

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, Pepperstone / Vantage / Fusion / BlackBull, 2022 to 2026.

What each hunter contributes. In four years of live-tick testing on four brokers, Skim placed about 60 % of all trades and won 17–20 % of them; the four Dives won 32–40 % of theirs (profit factor 2.5–4.3) and earned three-quarters of the money. Skim is the busy one; the Dives are the profitable ones. Two per-hunter inputs let you tune this without touching anything else: Inp<Hunter>RiskWeight scales that hunter's position size (1.0 = as tested; 0.25 = a quarter), and Inp<Hunter>GateStopAtr gives that hunter its own quiet-market threshold (0 = use the global 0.20), and Inp<Hunter>GateActivity makes that hunter stand aside while the market is calm at the minute scale (average one-minute range of the last 15 minutes divided by the last four hours; 1.0 = only trade when the last quarter hour is at least as active as the afternoon behind it; 0 = off). In four years of testing, Skim's fills that arrived on a calm quarter hour won 12–14 % — below breakeven — and its fills on an active one won 20–25 %. The tested configuration keeps all five at weight 1.0 and the activity gates off.

Every position opens with a stop loss of 2.00 and a take profit of 15.00 (14.00 for Dive 2) in account currency per 0.01 lot. Nothing is averaged, nothing is added to a loser, no grid, no martingale.

Two things make it different from a plain breakout robot:

  1. It knows when to stay out. The stop loss is a fixed amount of money. When gold is quiet and the hourly range is small, a 2.00 stop is inside the noise and gets hit before the move starts — that is where breakout robots bleed. KINGFISHER trades only while the stop is at most 20 % of the hourly range. In a quiet month it may place a handful of orders; in a quiet year, almost none.
  2. It reads how the fill happened. A fill that arrives on a fast push is allowed to ride to the full target. A fill that arrives slowly is handled by the Grip: once the trade is 1.00 ahead per 0.01 lot, KINGFISHER tracks the best profit and closes the trade if it gives back half of it.

2. Installation (five minutes)

  1. Open MetaTrader 5. File → Open Data Folder, then open MQL5\Experts. Copy KINGFISHER.ex5 there (or into any subfolder).
  2. In MetaTrader, right-click the Navigator panel → Refresh. KINGFISHER appears under Expert Advisors.
  3. Open a XAUUSD chart. Any timeframe is fine — KINGFISHER reads the 4-hour, 1-hour and 15-minute bars itself. The M15 chart is what all the testing used.
  4. Drag KINGFISHER onto the chart. In the window that opens, tick Allow Algo Trading on the Common tab. On the Inputs tab only section 1. Start here needs you (chapter 2a); the defaults are the validated configuration. Click OK.
  5. Make sure the Algo Trading button in the toolbar is green. A smiling face in the chart's top-right corner means the EA is running.

The journal (Toolbox → Experts) shows one line on start, for example:

KINGFISHER v1.11 | account Personal | settings Recommended | XAUUSD | 5 of 5 hunters | risk Medium (1.00% per level) | exit Smart, Skim Grip | trade while stop <= 0.20 x hourly ATR | max lot 5.00 | THE AEGIS ARCADE
KINGFISHER sizing: on a balance of 10000.00 a Dive order is 0.12 lot (broker minimum 0.01)

If your account is a netting account (one position per symbol) the EA prints a note; see chapter 6.

Your broker's symbol may carry a suffix (XAUUSD+, XAUUSDp, GOLD.pro). That is fine — attach to whatever your broker calls spot gold against USD.

2a. The inputs at a glance (since 1.10)

The Inputs tab is ordered by how often you should touch it. Most users change only section 1.

Section What it holds Touch it?
1. Start here Account type · Strategy settings · Risk level · Size as if the balance were · Max spread yes - this is the whole setup
2. Hunters on or off the five hunters, the Rebound switch (off; chapter 7.3), buy/sell rarely
3. Exit [Custom] how trades close only with Strategy settings = Custom
4. Prop-firm rules your firm's limits (chapter 3b) prop accounts, or as a daily-loss floor on any account
5. When not to trade weekend times (your broker's clock); the quiet-market gates [Custom] weekend times only if your broker's clock differs
6. Safety maximum lot, margin cap, gold-only lock, magic number a second chart needs its own magic number

8.-13. Engine, Dive 1-4, Skim [Custom] every value of every hunter only with Strategy settings = Custom

Strategy settings (InpProfile) decides whether the [Custom] sections count:

  • Recommended (default) - the validated configuration. Every [Custom] input is ignored and the tested value is used, so a stray edit cannot change the machine. The four 4-hour Dives ride fast fills to the target; the Skim hunter locks its gains (the Grip, chapter 7). Measured best on the tight-fill brokers (Pepperstone, Vantage).
  • Classic - the 1.05-1.09 behaviour: Skim rides like the Dives. Measured better on a wide-fill feed (Fusion).
  • Custom - every [Custom] input counts. Custom with untouched inputs is exactly Recommended.

Size as if the balance were (InpVirtualBalance, 0 = off): the EA sizes on this figure instead of the balance, never above the account's equity - for example 2,000 on a 10,000 account, or the balance plus a broker credit.

Max spread (InpMaxSpreadUsd): new orders wait while the spread is wider than this many dollars (5.00 = effectively off for gold). Before 1.10 it was in points, which is ten times tighter on a broker quoting gold with three decimals.

Gold only: on a live chart that is not gold KINGFISHER loads, prints why, and places nothing (section 6 can allow other symbols; in the Strategy Tester it always runs, so the Market validator can test it).

The second Journal line says what your risk level buys at your balance, for example a Dive order is 0.01 lot (broker minimum 0.01) - the minimum lot sets the risk here. On a small account every risk level trades the same minimum lot until the balance grows (about 1,200 at Medium, 2,400 at Low).

3. The only setting that matters: risk level

InpRisk — one choice, four levels plus Custom.

Level Risk per level (Skim at a quarter of it) What the testing showed (three live-tick feeds, 2024–2026, 10,000 start)
Low 0.5 % of balance ×4–7 in 15–30 months at a 4–5 % worst drawdown
Medium (default) 1.0 % ×17–22 in 15–30 months; worst drawdown 8–10 % (Pepperstone ×20 / 9.3 %, Vantage ×22 / 7.9 %, Fusion ×17 / 10.2 %)
High 1.5 % ×27–53; worst drawdown 12–15 % (Pepperstone ×27 / 12.1 %, Fusion ×53 / 15.3 %)
Hot 2.0 % drawdown can exceed 20 % of balance — experienced users, risk capital only

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, Pepperstone / Vantage / Fusion, 2024 to 2026.

Version 1.04 changed the default split: Skim (the busy 1-hour hunter, 60 % of trades) now carries a quarter of a level's budget and the four Dives (profit factor 2.5–4.3) the full one. At the same drawdown this compounds faster on every broker tested; the old 1.02 behaviour is InpS1RiskWeight = 1.0 with InpRisk = Low.

Which exits these figures used. The table was measured with the Classic exits (1.05-1.09). With the 1.10 default (Recommended: Skim on Grip) at Medium: Pepperstone x20.6 at a 5.3 % worst drawdown (same growth, drawdown down 43 %), Vantage x15.8 at 5.9 % (smoother, slower on a 10,000 account), Fusion x11.2 at 12.4 % (choose Classic there); BlackBull is being re-measured on 1.10. On a small account Recommended is both smoother and faster (chapter 7).

"Per level" means: when the four Dives fill on the same break, together they risk that percentage — not four times it. That is the setting that turns the 40 % drawdowns of per-order sizing into the 8–15 % above.

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, Pepperstone / Vantage / Fusion / BlackBull, 2022 to 2026 (bootstrap resample of the same four-broker backtest history).

What you should expect at each account size (Medium):

  • 10,000 and up: the numbers in the table.
  • 1,000: one 0.01 lot already risks 2.00 per hunter; the EA never trades below the broker minimum, so the real risk per level is about 0.8 % — the drawdown will be proportionally larger (up to ~16 %).
  • 500: about 1.6 % per level; a 2025-type stretch can produce a 20 % drawdown. Start on Low.
  • 100: the minimum lot rules everything — each fill risks $2.10 (2.1 %), a four-Dive burst 8.4 %. Replayed on four brokers' real ticks a $100 account never hit a stop-out at 1:200 or higher, reached $1,000 in 98–100 % of resampled histories, and grew to $2,600–13,000 in 1.2–3.5 years — but it fell to $57–92 first, and in a fair-game year (2025-type) one history in three dips below $50. Leverage matters here: at 1:100 the broker refuses part of each burst for margin and the stop-out sits at ~$44; at 1:30 a $100 account cannot open a single 0.01 lot (about $300 is the floor). Above ~$400 the percentage sizing takes over and the drawdown falls back to the 10–15 % of the table.

The honest sentence. These are backtests on real broker ticks, not results from live trading. KINGFISHER made its money in one kind of market — gold moving in large daily bursts, the way it has since late 2025. In quiet years (2023, 2024 on the four-year feed) it placed 18 trades in two years and finished flat. It should not lose much in a quiet year; it will not earn much either. Do not run it for income you need next month.

3a. Prop-firm accounts: one choice (since 1.08)

The first input, Account type (InpAccountMode), is all a prop-firm account needs:

Account type What it does
Personal (default) the risk level of chapter 3 applies
Prop firm 2-step — for challenges with a 5 % daily and 10 % maximum loss (FTMO-class) size fixed at 0.5 % of balance per level (the Low level); orders removed before every weekend
Prop firm 1-step — for challenges with a 3 % daily and 6 % maximum loss, and for any challenge on a standard-spread account size fixed at 0.25 % per level; orders removed before every weekend

Nothing else to set: the account type overrides the risk level, and the first line of the Journal names the one that is running (KINGFISHER v1.08 | account Prop firm 2-step | ...).

Simulation on backtest history, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, BlackBull / Vantage / OANDA, 2023 to 2026 (day-block bootstrap over the tested trade record; see the table below).

Why the size is fixed. The shipped Medium level is built for a personal account. On a 5 %/10 % two-step challenge it passed only about 4 attempts in 5 and lost about 1 funded account in 7 within 90 days, because a single fast day can cost more than 5 % at that size. The prop sizes below are the measured answer.

What the measurements say — a simulation that replays the backtest's own days in random blocks (10,000 paths, the challenge's rules applied tick-close by tick-close); a statement about the tested history, not a prediction:

broker type (feed, window) account type challenge passes per attempt median trading days to funded funded account lost within 90 days
raw spread (BlackBull, 2023–2026) Prop firm 2-step 5 % / 10 %, 10 % then 5 % target 98.5 % 117 (42 in the active 2025–26 market) 0.5 %
raw spread (Vantage, 2025–2026) Prop firm 2-step same 100 % 34 0 %
standard spread (~$0.25, OANDA-class, 2025–2026) Prop firm 2-step same 73 % 96 22 %
standard spread (same) Prop firm 1-step same 96 % 204 0.1 %
raw spread (BlackBull, 2023–2026) Prop firm 1-step 3 % / 6 %, 10 % target 97.6 % 132 0.1 %

Read the table as three rules:

  1. Raw-spread account (gold spread about $0.10 or less): choose Prop firm 2-step for a 5 %/10 % challenge.
  2. Standard-spread account, or a tighter challenge: choose Prop firm 1-step. The wider spread consumes a larger share of the fixed 2.00 stop; the same entries are stopped out more often (chapter 8), so the smaller size is the one that survives the daily limit.
  3. It can take months. KINGFISHER stands aside in quiet markets (chapter 6). In a quiet stretch it may place few trades for weeks; a challenge without a time limit is the right kind.

What the EA does not do for you — check your firm's rules before you start:

  • it does not read the firm's news calendar; some funded accounts forbid opening or closing trades a few minutes around high-impact releases;
  • it keeps its own count of the firm's daily and maximum loss (chapter 3b: the prop account types fill in 5 %/10 % or 3 %/6 %, and you can type your firm's numbers); the size is what keeps a normal bad day far inside it;
  • trades often last seconds to minutes; firms that set a minimum trade duration are not suitable;
  • one chart, one instance (a second chart needs its own magic number, chapter 12).

Source of every figure: the project's research journal (entries J11, J19, J21, J29) and scripts/_prop_sim.py (FTMO-class rules, day-block bootstrap), cross-checked against an independent prop-firm simulator within one percentage point.

3b. Prop-firm rules: your firm's own numbers (since 1.09)

The Account type (chapter 3a) chooses the size. The group right under it, Prop-firm rules, enforces your firm's limits. Choosing Prop firm 2-step or Prop firm 1-step already fills in the two loss limits for you (5 % daily and 10 % maximum, or 3 % and 6 %; both fixed, and the EA acts 0.5 % before each); type your firm's own numbers to override them. With Personal every rule is off until you type a number.

When a limit is about to be reached, the EA closes its own trades, deletes its own orders and stops opening new ones: until the firm's next day for the daily rules, for good for the maximum loss and the profit target. The Journal says which rule acted and at what equity:

KINGFISHER prop-firm rule: daily loss limit (equity 95512.40, day start 100020.00, firm limit 95020.00) - trades closed, orders deleted, paused until the firm's next day

and the start line lists what is on:

KINGFISHER prop-firm rules: account size 100000.00 | daily loss 5.00% of account size from the higher of day-start balance and equity - the EA acts at 4.50% of account size | maximum loss 10.00% (fixed) - the EA acts at 9.50% | ...

The inputs (group "4. Prop-firm rules (0 = rule off)")

Input What to enter Default
Account size (InpPropAccountSize) the challenge's account size, e.g. 100000 (0 = the balance when the EA first starts) 0
Daily loss limit % (InpPropDailyLossPct) the firm's daily loss, e.g. 5 0 = off; a prop account type fills in 5 or 3
Daily loss limit is a % of (InpPropDailyPctOf) Account size (the same amount every day - most firms) or the day-start value Account size
Daily loss is counted from (InpPropDailyFrom) the value at the start of the firm's day: the higher of balance and equity (strictest, safe for every firm), balance or equity the higher of the two
Maximum loss limit % (InpPropMaxLossPct) the firm's maximum loss, e.g. 10 0 = off; a prop account type fills in 10 or 6
Maximum loss limit type (InpPropMaxRule) Fixed (account size minus the limit - most two-step challenges), Trailing: highest day-start balance, Trailing: highest equity, live Fixed
A trailing limit stops rising at the account size (InpPropTrailLock) most trailing firms stop moving the limit once it reaches the starting balance yes

Hour the firm's day starts (InpPropDayStartHour) the hour on your platform's clock (the Market Watch time) at which the firm restarts its daily loss 0

Examples - what to type

From the LuxAlgo prop-firm directory on 23 September 2026 (the daily-loss wording is inferred by that directory; your firm's own rules page is always the authority, and firms change their rules):

Challenge (100,000 account) Daily loss % Maximum loss % and type Profit target %
FTMO Normal, 2-step 5 10, Fixed 10, then 5

All six count the daily loss as a share of the account size from the previous day's closing balance, open trades included. Leave counted from at the higher of balance and equity - it is never later than the firm's own meter.

Profit target: set it for the challenge phases only. When the target is reached the EA stops for good on that login; on the funded account set it back to 0.

The firm's day. Most firms restart the daily loss at midnight of the platform's own clock: leave 0. If your firm names another time, convert it to the Market Watch clock. Gold is closed for about an hour around the broker's midnight on most platforms, so for gold an hour's difference rarely matters; a market that trades through midnight (Bitcoin) needs the exact hour.

Restarts. The EA remembers the account size, the day's starting value, the highest balance and whether a rule stopped it, across restarts of the platform (terminal global variables named PR<login>_<magic>_...). A restart in the middle of the day does not give it a fresh daily allowance.

What these rules do not do

  • They close this EA's trades only. The limits are measured on the whole account's equity, so trades from another EA or by hand count toward them; give every EA on the account the same numbers.
  • The EA acts on the first price tick past its limit. A gap or a very fast market can carry the loss a little further before the closing trades fill - that is what the safety margin is for; do not set it to 0 on a real challenge.
  • No news filter. Some funded accounts forbid opening or closing trades a few minutes around high-impact releases. The platform's economic calendar cannot be replayed in the Strategy Tester, so a news rule could not be proved the way every rule above was; pause the EA by hand around those releases if your firm requires it.
  • No minimum trade duration and no minimum number of trading days.

Proof. With every rule off, this version placed the same deals as the version before it, deal for deal (82 trades, net +884.24, Pepperstone real ticks, 28 August - 19 September 2026). Each rule was then switched on at a level the same weeks reach; at all 19 triggers the EA closed its trades, opened nothing until the pause ended, and placed exactly the same deals as with the rules off up to the first trigger.

4. Your first week

  1. Run it on a demo account first, same broker, same settings, for at least four weeks. Nothing in this manual replaces watching it on your own broker's prices.
  2. On the demo, note two numbers each day from the account history: - Fills per day. In an active market, 2–4. In a quiet week, zero is normal — that is the gate working, not a fault. - How far past the stop loss the losers closed. The stop is placed at 2.00; on the tested brokers the median loser closed at 2.06–2.30 and one in ten at 3.45–4.45. If your broker's median is past 2.60, your costs are materially worse than anything tested — stay on demo or change broker.
  3. When you go live, start at Low for the first eight trading weeks, or set Custom with InpRiskPct = 0.25.
  4. Move to Medium only after at least 60 closed trades with a profit factor above 1.5 and no single day worse than −3 % of balance.

5. Reading the chart and the journal

KINGFISHER draws nothing on the chart. You will see:

  • Pending orders (dotted lines): buy stops above price, sell stops below, comment KF D1 … KF D4 (the Dives) and KF S1 (Skim). Up to three per hunter and side.
  • Open positions with a stop loss and take profit line each.
  • Orders disappearing — normal: they expire after each hunter's patience, they are removed on Friday evening, and they are cancelled when the hourly range shrinks below the gate.

Journal lines you may see:

Line Meaning
KINGFISHER v1.04 … started; the banner repeats the risk level and exit mode
Note: this account is in netting mode … see chapter 6
Note: Hot risk level … you chose Hot; the warning is deliberate
KINGFISHER: orders placed N \| skipped - quiet hour … printed when the EA is removed; a summary of why orders were or were not placed

6. When it does not trade — and why that is usually right

Situation What happens Setting
Quiet market: 2.00 stop > 20 % of the hourly range no new orders; resting orders are cancelled InpGateStopAtr (0 = trade always)
Weekend orders removed Friday 19:55 broker time; new orders from Monday 05:01 InpWeekendShield, InpFriCutoff*, InpMonResume*
Spread wider than 500 points order not placed InpMaxSpreadPoints
Broker rejects for margin order skipped, counted in the summary InpMaxRestingMarginPct; see below
Hunter already in a trade that hunter places nothing until it closes by design
Level is broken or price is past it no order until a new unbroken level forms by design

Netting accounts. KINGFISHER rests several orders at once. On a netting account with a small balance the broker may reject some of them for margin. Set InpMaxRestingMarginPct to, say, 60: the EA then refuses to add an order when the margin of open positions plus all resting orders would exceed 60 % of equity. On hedging accounts this is rarely needed (0 = off, the tested setting).

News. KINGFISHER has no news filter. Its stop loss is fixed and always on the server; a news spike either fills the order and runs to the target, or stops it out at 2.00 plus slippage. The tested numbers include every news event of 2024–2026.

7. Exit modes

Since 1.10 the default (Strategy settings = Recommended) is: the four Dives on Smart, the Skim hunter on Grip. The Skim hunter's fills arrive with less push and most of its losses were full 2.00 stops; the Grip lets it keep its small gains instead. On a small account this matters most, because there Skim trades the same minimum lot as a Dive (its quarter weight cannot go below the broker minimum). Measured on Vantage, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, 600 balance, 7 July - 18 September 2026:

600 balance, Vantage net days in profit worst day worst drawdown
Recommended (Skim on Grip) +558 (+93 %) 70 % -12.0 15.9
Classic (Skim rides) +500 (+83 %) 43 % -21.6 54.1
Skim switched off +401 (+67 %) 50 % -15.5 31.5

Over 18 months at the minimum lot (Pepperstone): net 3,129 vs 3,194, worst drawdown 47 vs 82, days in profit 62 vs 50 %. On a wide-fill feed (Fusion) the Grip's small wins are eaten by the fill cost - there choose Classic.

InpExitMode (section 3, Strategy settings = Custom):

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, Pepperstone / Vantage / Fusion, 2024 to 2026 (comparative ranking across exit modes).

  • Smart (default). The fill is read on the first tick after it opens. If price arrived on a push (the last 5 seconds moved at least 4.5 % of the hourly range — InpSmartPushAtr), the trade rides to the 15.00 target. Otherwise the Grip manages it.
  • Grip every trade. The Grip on all trades: once 1.00 ahead per 0.01 lot (InpGripStart), close if profit falls to 50 % of its best (InpGripKeepPct). Highest win rate, lowest net — it sells the big winners.
  • Target only. Hold every trade to the stop or the target. Highest net, widest drawdown.

In the testing, Smart kept Target-only's profit at about a quarter less drawdown, on four brokers.

Which exit compounds fastest depends on your broker's stop fills. Where the losers fill close to the $2.00 stop (Vantage, Pepperstone in our tests), Grip every trade at the High level was the smoothest and fastest path (Vantage: ×27 in 15 months at a 5.7 % worst drawdown; Pepperstone ×20 at 7.0 %). Where the fills are wide (Fusion), the Grip's small wins are eaten by the fill cost and Smart at the High level is the fast path (×53 at 15.3 %). Run four weeks on demo, read the median fill distance past $2.00 (chapter 4): under ~$2.15 → Grip; above → Smart.

7.0 Since 1.11: on a high-volatility day the Skim hunter rides

The Grip on the Skim hunter pays in quiet and ordinary markets, where a break stalls and Grip keeps what a ride would give back. On days whose volatility is in the top third of the past year (yesterday's daily range against its own last 250 days), breaks run far, and gripping at half the best profit cuts the runners - there the Classic exit earns more. From 1.11 the Skim hunter uses the Exit section's mode (Smart) on those days and Grip on all others. Nothing to set; it follows the market by itself.

Medium, 10,000, April 2025 - September 2026, backtest (BlackBull / Fusion) growth worst drawdown
BlackBull, 1.10 (Grip always) x13.6 8.8 %
BlackBull, Classic (ride always) x18.0 16.1 %
BlackBull, 1.11 (ride on high-volatility days) x18.8 8.8 %
Fusion, 1.10 / Classic / 1.11 x11.0 / x16.3 / x16.2 8.8 % / 11.5 % / 8.8 %

On a small account the switch changes nothing in quiet months: on 600 at the minimum lot from 7 July to 18 September 2026 it placed exactly the same deals as 1.10 on two brokers, because no day reached the top third.

On a 600 account at the minimum lot through the volatile October 2025 - May 2026 stretch, 1.11 made +2,346 against +1,471 for 1.10, with smaller dips (worst five days -43.7 against -58.7 per 0.01 lot). Proved before release: 1.11 places exactly the same deals as the tested configuration (524 trades, +24,186.66 on IC Markets real ticks, April - September 2026, where 1.10 made +23,451.24), and the Classic and prop settings carry it.

7.1 One exit per hunter (InpD1Exit … InpS1Exit, since 1.05)

Each hunter can override the global exit: Global (the default: whatever InpExitMode says), Smart, Grip every trade or Target only. The one that matters is the Skim hunter (1-hour level). Its fills arrive with less push than the 4-hour fills and lose more often at the stop (57–66 % of its trades under Smart); on the Grip it closes 35 % at the stop and keeps most of the rest as small wins. Whether that pays depends, again, on your broker's stop fills:

7.2 The smoothest path on a tight-fill broker: presets/KINGFISHER_high_grip.set

High level with Grip every trade. On Vantage (2025-06 → 2026-09, 10,000 start) it was the fastest and the shallowest of every arm tested: ×27.0 at a 5.7 % worst drawdown, profit factor 3.83, 72 % of trades closed in profit; at a fixed 0.01 lot it closes 28 % of trades at the stop against 62 % for Smart, and its worst 30-day loss is 14.00 per 0.01 lot against 17.00. Do not use this preset on a small account. Below about 2,000 every fill is the 0.01 minimum lot whatever the risk level says, so the Grip's advantage — which is that it lets you run a larger risk fraction for the same drawdown — cannot be taken, while its cost is paid in full. Measured on a 600 balance over 3 August to 18 September 2026 at the minimum lot: compiled defaults made 250 (Pepperstone) and 365 (Vantage) while this preset made 145 and 224, because the month's profit came from a few large pushed moves and the Grip sells exactly those. On a small account run the compiled defaults, or the per-hunter setting in 7.1, and come back to this preset when the balance is large enough for the risk level to change the lot size. The 15-month Vantage replay from 600 at 1:500 that reaches 10,606 assumes the balance grows past the minimum-lot floor early and stays there; it is a statement about the whole path, not about the first months, which is where the paragraph above applies.

7.3 Since 1.12: Rebound - reverse after a Skim stop-out (opt-in, InpRebound, off by default)

A Skim order is a bet that the 1-hour level breaks. When it is stopped out, the break has failed: price went through the level, turned, and came back through the Skim's entry. Traders who bought that break have their stops just inside the level, and when those stops fill they push price further back into the range. Rebound trades that return leg. When a Skim trade closes at its stop, KINGFISHER places one opposite stop order 2.50 beyond the Skim's entry (0.50 past the Skim's stop):

  • stop 1.00 beyond the furthest point the Skim trade reached, so the order does not sit where the failed break just turned;
  • target 15.00 per 0.01 lot, the same as the Skim;
  • size set so the order risks the same amount of money as the Skim trade it follows;
  • life 3 hours. It is also removed early if a 15-minute candle pushes hard back in the Skim's direction (a body at least the 15-minute ATR(14)); while it waits, its stop follows a new Skim extreme.

It never hedges. If any KINGFISHER trade opens the other way, a waiting Rebound order is deleted, and an open Rebound trade is closed. The Skim hunter keeps trading as before: switching Rebound on removes none of its trades. A Skim closed by the Grip or by Smart gets no Rebound. Neither does a Dive: both were tested and neither held up.

Medium, 10,000, April 2025 - September 2026, real-tick backtest profit with Rebound, against off worst drawdown
BlackBull +14 % about the same (x0.97)
Fusion +19 % 19 % smaller (x0.81)
OANDA (wide spread) -5 % -
Dukascopy gold history 2013 - 2015, replayed at today's price flat (-0.1 %) about the same

Switch it on only if all three are true:

  1. Your broker fills tight. The Rebound order sits close to the Skim's stop, so the spread is a large share of what it can earn. It paid on the two raw-spread brokers and lost on the wide-spread feed. Use the four-week fill test in chapter 4.
  2. Your account is well above the minimum lot. The Rebound stop is at least 3.50 away and usually more. At 0.01 lot it therefore risks more than the Skim did. It was not tested on a small account: leave it off below about 2,000.
  3. You accept a slower quiet stretch. On BlackBull the Rebound trades were behind in the quiet summer of 2025 (May - October). The dips were deeper there before they led from November on. On Fusion the dips were no deeper at any time.

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, BlackBull / Fusion / OANDA / Dukascopy, 2015 to 2025 (fresh out-of-sample windows).

What it is not: a proven law. Before 2025 the same trades were flat to negative. On 53 stop-out Rebounds in earlier gold windows never used to build it (2015, 2018 - early 2025) the average was -0.28 of the amount risked. The gain above belongs to the volatile gold market of 2025 - 2026. It is a choice for traders who expect that market to continue, which is why it ships off.

Proved before release: with Rebound off, 1.12 places exactly the same deals as 1.11. With it on, 1.12 places exactly the same deals as the research build the table above was measured on, including under the Prop 2-step account type.

8. Execution quality — what your broker adds

Same entries, same hit rate on every broker tested; what differs is how far past the 2.00 stop you are filled on a fast move. Measured medians (2026, losers): 2.06, 2.11, 2.17, 2.30 on the four live feeds; the widest feed earned about 25 % less per trade than the tightest. Execution delay of 100–500 ms was tested and changed the result by at most 1 %; it is the broker's quote path, not latency, that costs.

Spread is the bigger cost, and it is visible before you trade. KINGFISHER's stop is a fixed 2.00 per 0.01 lot, so the spread takes a fixed share of it. Measured on the same entries on two brokers (2025–26): on a feed whose gold spread was about $0.24 at the median, 8.5 % of fills were stopped out within one second of the fill (0.1–3.0 % on raw-spread feeds) and the shared entries earned about half as much per trade (0.99 against 2.08 per 0.01 lot). Across four feeds, fills taken while the spread was at most 3 % of the stop earned the most on every feed.

Choose a broker with tight gold spreads (a raw-spread / ECN account) and fast stop execution. Prefer a hedging account. The input InpMaxSpreadPoints (500) is in points: 500 points is a 5.00 spread on a two-decimal gold quote and 0.50 on a three-decimal one — look at your symbol's digits before relying on it.

9. Sizing and margin

Backtest, MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester, real ticks, Pepperstone / Vantage / Fusion / BlackBull, 2024 to 2026 (per-order vs per-level risk sizing comparison).

  • The size of each order is computed from the risk level, the balance, the 2.00 stop and the broker's tick value, then shared among the hunters on that level (LOT_RISK_PER_LEVEL).
  • InpMaxLot (5.0) caps every order. In the tests it only came into play after the balance had grown twenty-fold.
  • Custom mode also offers fixed lots (LOT_FIXED + InpFixedLot), lots per 1,000 of balance, and risk per order (each fill risks the full percentage — in the same tests this took the drawdown past 40 %; not recommended).
  • Leverage: 1:100 or higher is comfortable. At 1:30 and Medium the resting orders can exhaust free margin on small accounts; use Low or set InpMaxRestingMarginPct.

10. Optimising (for advanced users)

Every value that moves a trade is an input, so the Strategy Tester's genetic optimiser can search any of them: the five hunters' offsets, stops, targets, patience, swing width and timeframe; the gate; the exit thresholds; the weekend times. Two warnings from the people who built it:

  • The defaults are the values that survived out-of-sample tests, random-control tests and four brokers. A parameter that looks better on last quarter alone is usually curve-fitting.
  • The first thing worth optimising is not an offset but the per-hunter risk weights (InpS1RiskWeight in particular) — they change how the risk budget is split between the busy hunter and the profitable ones, and a change there is a sizing decision, not a curve fit.
  • Optimise on real ticks ("Every tick based on real ticks"), never on OHLC — the entries depend on the tick path.

11. What is saved and what is not

KINGFISHER keeps no files. After a restart it re-adopts its own orders and positions by magic number and comment. The Grip's "best profit so far" for an open trade is not saved: after a restart it starts tracking again from the moment the trade is next seen, and the Smart read of "fast or slow fill" is taken again on that first tick.
