What we are building, and what is still to do

This page tracks the Expert Advisors we are building or rebuilding, one line per system, and says plainly which steps are finished and which are not. A system appears here before it has a record that meets our publishing bar, so there are no performance figures on this page. When a system earns a product page, it moves to the Products section with its figures and the broker and period they come from.

How we judge a build

A rebuild is not finished when its trades look like the reference. We check it in seven steps, and this page shows where each system stands on each.

  • Entries and exits reproduce the behaviour we are rebuilding, trade for trade where we can.
  • Exit tuning is a separate step. Exit settings are optimised on purpose, on the longest history we have and on the alternative price paths below, and the result is checked on data that was not used for tuning. Matching the reference is not tuning, and until a system has this step its exits are only as good as the reference's.
  • Extended history means the longest tick history we hold, on more than one broker feed.
  • Other price paths make the same market harder: older years, prices rescaled to today's level, the path inverted, the path run backwards, and inverted and backwards together. A real edge survives these. A fitted one does not, and we would rather learn that here than live.
  • Risk sizing and forward testing come last: lot size by percent of balance with a ruin and recovery profile, then a demo account on live prices.

What each column means
  • Entries. The rule that starts a trade is written down and reproduces the reference behaviour.
  • Exits. The stop, target, trail and closing rules are written down and reproduce the reference behaviour.
  • Exit tuning. Exit settings optimised on purpose, on the long history and on the alternative data below, with the result checked on data not used for tuning. Matching the reference is not tuning.
  • Extended history. Tested on the longest tick history we hold on more than one broker feed, including the years a system was not built on.
  • Other price paths. Tested on the same market made harder: older years, prices rescaled to today's level, the path inverted, the path run backwards, and inverted and backwards together. A real edge survives these; a fitted one does not.
  • Risk sizing. Lot size by percent of balance written and compared with the reference, then checked against a ruin and recovery profile.
  • Forward test. Running on a demo account on live prices after the build, before any sale.

What is still pending, system by system

Sanderling Gold

Four small engines in one: buying and selling daily short-term extremes with an indicator-based exit, a fade on the hourly chart, and a four-hour continuation entry that rides a trailing stop.

Where it stands: First version built and compared with its reference on two broker feeds.

  • Exit tuning has not been done. The exits match the reference; they have not been optimised, on the long history or on any alternative data.
  • Other price paths not yet run: the older tick years, prices rescaled to today's level, inverted, backwards, and inverted and backwards.
  • Percent-of-balance sizing and the ruin and recovery profile.
  • Two of the four engines match their reference only partly; their entry filters are still being refined.
  • A demo forward test.

Lapwing Gold

Builds the overnight range, then takes the first break of it in the direction of the larger trend, with the stop on the far side of the range and a fixed multiple as the target. One trade a day, flat before the close.

Where it stands: First version built and compared with its reference on two broker feeds.

  • Exit tuning has not been done: target multiple, stop placement, closing time and a break-even step have only been matched to the reference, not optimised.
  • Other price paths not yet run (older years, rescaled, inverted, backwards, inverted and backwards).
  • A cap on very wide overnight ranges and one further day filter are not built yet.
  • Lot-size calibration against the reference and a ruin and recovery profile.
  • A demo forward test.

Merlin Gold

Waits for a pullback into one of two long moving averages and trades back out of it.

Where it stands: Entry level found; the rule that chooses which pullbacks to trade is not written yet.

  • The selection rule for which pullbacks are traded.
  • Exits, then everything after them.

Curlew Gold

Trades only in the direction of a slow trend, with short holds of about half a day.

Where it stands: Direction rule written; entry timing and exits are not.

  • Entry timing inside the trend.
  • The exit rule.
  • Everything after them.

Fieldfare Gold

A trend system that reverses between long and short and adds to winners.

Where it stands: Structure understood; its direction state is a blend of trend measures that is not written yet.

  • The direction rule.
  • A learned or composite state may be involved; if so it would be built as a trained model, not a fixed rule.
  • Everything after that.

Your question: has proper exit optimisation been applied?

Not yet, for any system on this page. The first versions reproduce the exits of the systems they are modelled on, and that has been checked on two broker feeds. None has had a deliberate exit optimisation, on the long history or on the alternative price paths, and none has been through the inverted and backwards tests yet. That is the next block of work after each system has its first version, and this page will show it as done when it is.
